Renting a truck in Denver: where it parks on moving day

The truck is the easy part. The curb is where moves lose an hour. Sort this a week ahead.

Step 1. Decide where the truck will actually sit

  • Your own driveway or a private lot: no city paperwork needed for the street, but check your landlord, HOA or building rules.
  • A metered space or the curb lane: you may need the city to reserve it.
  • An alley: ask the city before you assume it is fine to block.

Step 2. If you are inside the City and County of Denver

Denver’s transportation department (DOTI) says short-term use of the public right-of-way takes a street occupancy permit submitted online through E-Permits. It points to a separate online meter bagging system for keeping meters clear; requests take up to 72 hours to process and a week of lead time is suggested. Source: DOTI Street Occupancy.

Honest gap: that page never mentions moving trucks or storage containers by name, and we could not find a DOTI page that does. Phone DOTI, describe your truck and block, and ask exactly what to file. The same page lists a permitting freeze from November 26, 2026 through January 3, 2027, so raise that if your date falls in it.

Step 3. Outside Denver

Aurora, Lakewood, Arvada, Westminster and the other metro cities each set their own curb rules. Call the city’s public works or parking office.

Step 4. Pick the right size

Rental companies publish a size guide with each truck. Use theirs, not ours, and when your list sits between two sizes, go bigger; a second trip costs more than the extra rental.

Step 5. Containers (PODS-style)

A container on the street can be treated like a truck for permit purposes. Ask the container company who is responsible for the permit and get the answer in writing.

Step 6. Day-before checks

  • Walk the route from door to truck and remove rugs and low-hanging clutter.
  • Pick up the truck early, when lots are quiet.
  • Have a dolly, straps and pads on the truck, or confirm the crew brings its own.
