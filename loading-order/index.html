Loading order: what goes on the truck first

If you pay by the hour, a good load plan is cash in your pocket. This is the order most crews work in. Adjust for your stuff.

  1. Prep before the crew arrives

    Boxes closed and taped, labeled by room, stacked at the door. Drawers either emptied or taped shut. Anything you will not ship set aside.

  2. Long and heavy, against the front wall

    Mattresses on edge, sofas, dressers and appliances go in first, against the wall behind the cab. Heavy at the front keeps the weight over the axle.

  3. Furniture in tight rows

    Tables with legs off, chairs seat to seat, wrapped in pads. Fill the floor first.

  4. Boxes on top of furniture, heaviest low

    Build a flat wall of boxes as high as you can without stacking more than you can reach. Heavy boxes at the bottom.

  5. Fragile and loose items last, near the door

    Lamps, mirrors, plants and the “open first” box come out first at the other end.

  6. Strap each layer

    Load straps across the load every few feet so nothing shifts on turns.

Protect floors and doorways

  • Lay runners or cardboard along the whole path.
  • Wrap door frames and banisters at tight corners with pads.
  • Furniture sliders help on hardwood and tile. Lift instead of dragging.
  • Put down a ramp board at the truck step so wheels do not dig into landscaping or asphalt.

Plan your hours

Crew hours go quickly when boxes are still open, hallways are crowded or the elevator has not been booked. Ask the crew lead how long they expect a given volume to take, then add a margin. See the cost ranges.
