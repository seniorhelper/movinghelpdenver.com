Stairs, heavy items and working at Denver’s altitude

Denver sits about a mile above sea level. If you or your helpers are visiting from lower ground, the third trip up the stairs feels longer than it should. Plan for it.

Pace yourself

  • Drink water before you are thirsty and keep a case on the truck.
  • Break every hour or so, in shade if you can.
  • Do the heaviest items early, when everyone is fresh.
  • Anyone with a heart or lung condition should talk to a doctor before heavy lifting here. That is general common sense, not medical advice from us.

Stairs

  1. Measure the stairwell, doors and turns before you buy or move a big piece. Walls win against furniture.
  2. Take off doors, legs and cushions to gain inches.
  3. Use two people on an item, one at each end, with a spotter at the bottom or landing.
  4. Lift with legs, keep the load close, and never carry something you cannot see around.
  5. Straps that loop around the lifters’ shoulders and the item help with narrow stairs. Crews often bring them; ask.

Items that justify hired help

  • Safes and gun safes
  • Pianos
  • Full-size refrigerators and washers, which also need a plan to disconnect safely
  • Sectionals and sleeper sofas

Weather, too

Denver weather can turn in an afternoon. Keep tarps and a few trash bags on the truck, and check the forecast the evening before so you can shift the start time if needed.

What to tell the crew when you request quotes

Floors, stairs and turns, parking distance, and anything over about 250 pounds. The more they know, the fewer surprises on the clock.
