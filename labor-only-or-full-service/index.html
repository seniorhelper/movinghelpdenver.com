Labor-only or full service? What each one costs and covers

Both put people on your stairs. The difference is who brings the truck, the pads and the insurance.

Side by side

Do the math before you pick

  1. Rental truck plus fuel plus mileage plus insurance add-ons.
  2. Crew hours multiplied by head count, including the minimum.
  3. Your own time and any pads or straps you have to buy.

Then compare with a full-service hourly quote. For small apartments labor-only often wins; for long hauls or big houses, it may not.

Is the crew legit?

Ask whether the company holds a Colorado PUC mover permit. Colorado rules say a mover’s advertising must carry its permit number and street address (4 CCR 723-6-6016), and the PUC’s page (puc.colorado.gov/movers) is the place to confirm. Our own rule is that crews must hold that permit, plus insurance and a BBB A+, before they get your job. If a labor-only company says the rules do not apply to it, ask for that in writing and think twice.
